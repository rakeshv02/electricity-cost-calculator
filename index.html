Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find the wattage of an appliance?

Check the label on the back or bottom of the appliance, or look it up in the manual. It's usually listed in watts (W) or kilowatts (kW). For computers, laptops, and monitors, it varies by usage — check the power adapter or use a plug-in energy monitor for accuracy.

What is the average electricity rate in the UK / US?

UK: approximately 24–29p/kWh (2024). US: approximately 13–16¢/kWh national average, but varies widely by state (Hawaii ~40¢, Louisiana ~10¢). Australia: approximately 25–35¢/kWh. Check your latest bill for your exact rate.

What uses the most electricity at home?

The biggest consumers are typically: electric heating (1,500–3,000W), tumble dryers (2,000–4,000W), ovens (2,000–5,000W), dishwashers (1,200–2,400W), washing machines (500–2,500W), and electric kettles (2,000–3,000W).

How can I reduce my electricity bill?

Switch to LED bulbs (use 75% less energy), upgrade to A-rated appliances, use a smart thermostat, run dishwashers/washing machines on eco/off-peak cycles, unplug devices on standby, and consider solar panels for long-term savings.

What is a kWh?

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the unit of energy on your electricity bill. It equals 1,000 watts used for one hour. For example, a 100W light bulb running for 10 hours uses 1 kWh.

What is the Electricity Cost Calculator?

The Electricity Cost Calculator estimates how much an appliance costs to run by combining its power draw, how long it runs, and your energy tariff. It's the quickest way to translate watts into pounds, dollars, or cents on your bill.

How it works

Energy use is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh). Convert an appliance's wattage to kilowatts (kW = watts ÷ 1000), multiply by hours used to get kWh, then multiply by your unit rate: Cost = (watts ÷ 1000) × hours × rate. Scale up by the number of days to see weekly, monthly, or annual cost.

Example 1 — A space heater. A 2,000 W heater run 4 hours a day at 28p/kWh: (2000 ÷ 1000) × 4 × 0.28 = £2.24/day, about £67/month.

Example 2 — A fridge. A 150 W fridge running effectively 8 hours a day at 16¢/kWh: (150 ÷ 1000) × 8 × 0.16 = $0.192/day, roughly $70/year.

When to use it

  • Deciding whether an old appliance is worth replacing with an efficient model.
  • Budgeting for high-draw devices like heaters, dryers, or EV chargers.
  • Spotting the biggest energy users in your home.
  • Comparing running costs before buying new equipment.
